Three discreet touch points, green, red and blue, sit on each side of the table. When two people each touch a point and touch each other, they close a circuit and trigger a melody with matching light. Seven states come out of the possible colour combinations, so the table stays open to interpretation rather than teaching one correct way to play it.

It builds on Hobye and Löwgren's idea of bare-skin touch: giving strangers a playful excuse to touch, mediated through an object rather than asked of them directly. Touch closes a circuit read by an Arduino Uno, which sends MIDI to a KORG Volca Keys synthesizer, and an LED strip lights up in the colour of the point that was touched.

We tested it with 13 pairs of students, five uninterrupted minutes each, then a short interview. Understanding the table took anywhere from five to forty-five seconds. Some pairs touched fingers and hands, others went ankle to ankle or forehead to forehead, and several tried to play a song together. A few noticed they could technically trigger it alone, and found that "boring", which is a sign it was doing its job of needing two people.
