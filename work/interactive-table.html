Interactive Sound-Mediating Table

A playful, ambiguous table that turns bare-skin touch into sound and light between strangers.

Role
Co-designer, with David Hagberg, Mafalda Gamboa and Sjoerd Hendriks
When
2024
Where
Chalmers University of Technology, Gothenburg
Type
Research and exhibition, physical prototype

Accepted and exhibited at ACM DIS 2024, demo track, Copenhagen.

The idea

Three discreet touch points, green, red and blue, sit on opposite sides of the table. When two people each touch a point, and touch each other, they close a circuit and trigger a melody and matching light. Seven states come out of the possible colour combinations, so the table stays open to interpretation rather than teaching one correct way to play it.

It builds on Hobye and Löwgren's idea of bare-skin touch: giving strangers a playful excuse to touch, mediated through an object rather than each other directly.

How it works

Touch closes a circuit read by an Arduino Uno, which sends a MIDI signal to a KORG Volca Keys synthesizer. The synthesizer's audio goes out through speakers, and an LED strip lights up in the colour of whichever point was touched.

Testing it

We deployed the table in a student environment and recruited 13 pairs of students, with a camera and microphone set up to record. Each pair got five uninterrupted minutes, followed by a semi-structured interview.

How long it took people to understand the table ranged from as quick as five seconds to as long as forty-five. Some pairs touched fingers and hands, others found more unusual contacts: ankle to ankle, forehead to forehead. Several started trying to play a song together.

“Blue feels a bit spooky. The others just feel like tones, white feels like classical music. Green feels Japanese, like sitting and drinking tea. Yellow feels a bit futuristic.”

Participants negotiated their own strategies for touch, some enjoyed the collaborative puzzle of it, and a few noted they could technically activate the table alone, but found that “boring”, a sign the design was doing its job of needing two people.

Outcome

The work was accepted to the ACM DIS 2024 demo track in Copenhagen, co-authored with David Hagberg, Mafalda Gamboa and Sjoerd Hendriks.
