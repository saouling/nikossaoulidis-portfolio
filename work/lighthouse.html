LightHouse

A stage-lighting app that makes a powerful, complex tool approachable, with a 3D stage preview and a visual identity of its own.

Role
UI designer, individual project
When
2024
Where
Graphical Computer Interfaces, Chalmers University of Technology
Type
UI design, prototyping, market research

Craft and clarity inside a genuinely complex tool.

The problem

Light rigs, the programmable lights used to enhance live shows, usually need complex software to control their dynamic effects. That complexity locks smaller venues and less experienced operators out of tools built for professionals.

What I did

I designed LightHouse to balance advanced functionality with intuitive usability, so it works for professional designers and beginners in smaller venues alike. It lets users group lights, build and manage sequences, and adjust colour, intensity and movement in real time, through an interface that stays streamlined rather than overwhelming.

For flexibility, it supports both a timeline-based programming mode and live control, backed by visual feedback through a detailed 3D stage preview.

Research & visual identity

I conducted market research into how existing lighting control solutions address user needs, and used the gaps I found to guide the design: an interface that prioritises clarity under real-world conditions like low light and loud environments. A modular workspace and quick-action tools serve the fast pace of live performance while staying usable for newcomers.

I also designed the app's visual identity, including the logo and branding, to match its modern, bold and minimalistic interface.

Outcome

By reducing cognitive load and focusing on an intuitive workflow, the goal was a tool that lets anyone craft an engaging light show, regardless of experience.
